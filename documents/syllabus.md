## Getting Help

At times, this class will be difficult. We cover topics that will be new to most of you, and it will ask you to think in ways you have not had to think before. Server code is often asynchronous, and there are frequently several layers of indirection between the line you typed and the thing that actually happened.

If you feel stuck, lost, or confused, you are in good company. I have spent plenty of time staring at my own code wondering what on earth it was doing.

This is a class, so it is a place to learn, and learning requires you to challenge yourself. What matters is what you do when you find yourself stuck. I am quite literally employed to help you understand this material, and beyond that, I want to.

When you hit a wall and can't get over it on your own in a reasonable amount of time, reach out to me. Use my office hours, send me an email, or ask in **#430-general** on Slack.

There is no shame in needing help. In a professional setting, your team lead will be far more frustrated that you sat stuck for three days than that you asked a question on day one.

## AI Policy

Utilizing AI tools (Copilot, Claude, ChatGPT, etc.) to generate code is in violation of the AI policy for this course and **will result in a 0** for the assignment. Repeat offenses will lead to more severe academic penalties up to and including **failure of the course**. You MAY use these tools for other tasks such as debugging, planning, and deepening your understanding.

**Rationale**: AI is becoming ubiquitous. Companies and developers are using these tools at ever increasing rates. Academically, these tools can be a real boon for learning when they are used properly. They can also become a crutch that limits your education severely.

The consistent message from industry is that these tools are only useful if you can vet what they produce. A large share of a developer's job now is reviewing generated code. To do that well, **you have to understand the code**, and that only happens if you spend the time learning how it all works.

I understand the temptation to generate large parts of your code. Doing so reduces your capacity to learn, which in turn wastes the considerable sum of money you are spending on your education.

If you do want to use these tools, ask yourself "how am I improving my understanding by doing this?" Having them explain a difficult concept or help you find a bug can be genuinely useful. They should not replace learning how to read documentation or search through Stack Overflow.
